# WILD CROWN: THE BROKEN PACT — English Narrative Content

## 1. Authority and scope

This is the English narrative adaptation of the original Game Design Bible, incorporating the approved decisions in `../01-MASTER-PLAN.md`. The original Bible remains unchanged. This document supplies narrative content, quest objectives, story beats, and the dialogue already present in the sources. It is not a complete voiceover script, a gameplay implementation, or evidence of runtime behavior.

Sources:

- `../../WILD-CROWN-GAME-DESIGN-BIBLE-CODEX-CLI.md`: premise (§1), world (§4), characters (§5), animal communication (§6), campaign (§7), final boss (§8), endings (§9), relationships (§17), side quests (§20), Grove progression (§21), and trailer copy (§40).
- `../01-MASTER-PLAN.md`: approved decisions (§0), canon reconciliation (§3), ending predicate (§4.6), campaign and side-quest ledger (§5), and absence-safe boss resolutions (§6).

The following approved corrections take precedence over conflicting Bible passages:

1. After Asha, Aren has **three** fragments: Sky, Canopy, and Fang.
2. If Solkar is exiled, a voluntary council representative can represent Crown. Exiled Solkar does not return automatically as a companion.
3. Koro's assistance in the curated First Pact slice is provisional cooperation, not proof that Aren has already earned his Bond.
4. Losing Warden's Call removes supernatural coercion, not all cooperation. Aren can still **Ask** and **Signal** willing companions.
5. Chapter 7 forms the **Wild March coalition**, not an automatic Living Pact ending.
6. Having used Dominion on any leader at any time in the current run permanently makes the Living Pact ending unavailable in that run. Later reconciliation does not erase that history.
7. Combat is nonlethal and contains no gore. Aren subdues poachers and cleanses or rescues animals when recovery is possible. Historical loss and natural predation are story context, not player killing mechanics.

Additional details explicitly identified as production-plan proposals retain that status here. Translation does not silently promote a proposal to original Bible canon. In particular, the five added side quests and the detailed numerical ending predicate are identified below. No new dialogue is invented to fill gaps.

## 2. Premise and narrative promise

Erya is tearing itself apart. Animals abandon their territories, rival communities hoard what little remains, and Black Sap turns fear, hunger, grief, and protective instinct into overwhelming rage. Aren, a young Warden raised by Yara, can hear the forest's memories and emotions. To restore Erya, Aren must discover why its creatures are fighting, confront corrupted leaders without killing them, and repair the conditions that made conflict possible.

The journey begins with a fallen bird and grows into an alliance of species. Birds reveal what Aren cannot see. Monkeys open routes through the canopy. A tiger protects the vulnerable. Wolves track and cooperate. A panther reveals hidden truths. The peoples of the plains confront the difference between leadership and control.

Aren can pursue trust through **Bond**, or impose obedience through **Dominion**. The central question is not whether the forest can be united, but whether that unity means anything if its creatures cannot refuse.

The Warden is a mediator, not a monarch. Cleansing Black Sap ends its emotional amplification; it does not conjure food, restore water by itself, settle politics, or manufacture trust.

## 3. World and lore

### Erya

Erya is an ancient, interconnected great forest. Its ecosystems include layered tropical forest, bamboo valleys, cold pine forests and mountains, mist-covered wetlands, central grasslands, and an underground network of roots and caverns. These distinct territories allow birds, monkeys, tigers, wolves, panthers, and lions to share one world without sharing one habitat.

The campaign begins at **Greenwake**, a settlement on Erya's edge. **The Grove** becomes Aren's sanctuary and meeting place. Five major regions surround it: **Whispering Canopy**, **Blood Bamboo Valley**, **Moonpine Ridge**, **Mistveil Basin**, and **Sunmane Expanse**. Beneath them lies **Heartroot Hollow**. The production plan places the Sky sanctuary and Canopy settlement within the same Whispering Canopy region; six factions do not require six separate regions.

### Heartroot

Heartroot is an immense ancient tree whose roots reach throughout Erya. It does not command the forest. It connects water, growth cycles, migration routes, instinctive memory, ecological energy, and the relationships between species. It is the forest's nervous system.

### The six Heartroot fragments

| Fragment | Represented group | Meaning | Campaign acquisition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sky | Birds | Vision | Vara brings the fragment to Aren in the prologue. |
| Canopy | Monkeys | Creativity and adaptation | Koro's resolution in Chapter 1. |
| Fang | Tigers | Strength used to protect | Asha's cleansing and reunion with Tali in Chapter 2. |
| Pack | Wolves | Unity | The pack settlement in Chapter 3; Sable becomes its companion envoy. |
| Shadow | Panthers | Instinct and hidden truth | The river's cleansing and Nyra's alliance in Chapter 4. |
| Crown | Lions and the represented plains communities | Leadership | Solkar's resolution in Chapter 5; council representation is valid after exile. |

No single leader is meant to possess all six powers. Aren's collection of them becomes the vulnerability Orun exploits.

### The Wild Pact

The Wild Pact allowed Wardens to sense animals, share memories, request help, mediate disputes, and cleanse imbalance. Its purpose was connection without ownership. No claw was to rule every trail, and no voice was to speak for every creature.

### The Red Summer

A prolonged drought dried the rivers. Herbivores left their territories, predators competed for prey, and leaders refused to share resources. War followed. Orun, the Warden of that era, lost his family and many of the creatures he had protected. He came to believe that freedom itself allowed the forest to destroy itself.

### The Root Crown

Orun made the Root Crown from Heartroot's roots and the power of the six fragments. He intended to merge every will into one, eliminating war, disputes, excessive hunting, disobedience, and imbalance.

His apprentice Yara helped him before understanding the cost. Once she realized that the Crown would erase freedom, she tried to stop the ritual. The ritual failed: Heartroot shattered into six fragments, Orun was pulled into the root network, and his fear and rage mingled with the sap. Black Sap was born. Orun became **the Hollow King**.

### Black Sap

Black Sap is more than a physical poison. It magnifies fear, anger, hunger, protective instinct, pain, and the desire for control. An infected animal has not ceased to be itself; its own emotions have become unbearable.

The production plan distinguishes recoverable corruption from irreversible tissue destruction when explaining Nyra's history. That distinction must be established through evidence and memories, not introduced arbitrarily to excuse killing a boss. It does not change Aren's nonlethal combat mandate.

### Bond and Dominion

Bond grows through rescuing members of a community, restoring habitats, respecting injured animals, cooperating appropriately, fulfilling commitments, and choosing solutions that respect a species' needs. It strengthens willing cooperation and resistance to Orun.

Dominion offers immediate obedience and early access to power. Its costs include diminished autonomy, a less lively sanctuary, and greater vulnerability to possession. The production plan makes possession a clearly signaled, nonlethal encounter with counterplay, not an unexplained random betrayal. Low Bond must not remove essential danger warnings or make the campaign impossible to finish.

The Living Pact restriction is historical: even one act of leader Dominion in the current run disqualifies that ending. It does not force the Dominion ending; Severance remains a choice.

## 4. Named character biographies

### Aren — the young Warden

Yara found Aren near a ruined part of Heartroot and raised them. Aren is neither royal nor descended from Orun. Heartroot chose Aren for the ability to listen without imposing a will on others.

At first, Aren believes troublemakers must be defeated, leaders gathered under one authority, and chaos resolved through unity. The journey challenges those assumptions: order is not justice, consent means little without the right to refuse, and leadership depends on trust rather than commands alone.

Aren uses Echo Sense, Wild Bond, Warden's Call, Root Tether, Cleanse Pulse, staff combat, and acrobatics. The Warden Staff shortens for movement and extends for combat; it supports strikes, vaults, counters, tethering, cleansing, and connection to the fragments. After Chapter 6, Ask/Signal replaces coercive Call while voluntary assistance remains possible.

Aren is the authored protagonist for the current production scope. The Bible's possible future name and gender customization is not a current feature commitment.

### Yara — mentor and keeper of a dangerous truth

Orun's last apprentice and Aren's guardian, Yara is both a mentor and a participant in the Crown's creation. Her concealment of that history damages Aren's trust. Her arc concerns responsibility and atonement, not merely revealing another person's wrongdoing. She eventually severs Orun's connection to Aren at the cost of becoming a living wooden statue. The Living Pact ending restores her.

### Vara — the scout

A stylized hornbill is the Bible's suggested species. Vara brings Sky to Aren and remains when Aren can no longer command. She represents vision: scouting, marking threats and weaknesses, warning of off-screen attacks, revealing concealed enemies, and reading pounce landings. Her loyalty establishes that companionship can survive the loss of supernatural authority.

### Koro — leader of the monkeys

Intelligent, suspicious, hostile toward hunters, and inclined to test strangers, Koro steals Aren's fragment during their first encounter. Hunger and poisoned water underlie his community's conflict with the birds. A Black Sap thorn in his shoulder worsens his anger.

Koro represents adaptation. He handles ropes, opens high routes, throws or drops objects, creates distractions, and helps reshape traversal. His provisional assistance in the First Pact slice must not be confused with a completed covenant. Cleansing him and restoring cooperation around water and seeds leads to his full utility-companion role.

### Asha — the mother tiger

One of Erya's strongest creatures, Asha is driven into rage after hunters take her cub, Tali. Black Sap traps intensify her protective instinct until she cannot recognize allies or even her child. Her recovery is a reunion, not a conquest.

Asha represents protective strength. She pounces, breaks armor and obstacles, roars, intercepts charges, and fights at the front. Her later commitment depends on the safety of Tali and the young, not on Aren treating her as a weapon.

### Tali — the tiger cub

Tali is not a combat companion. After the main rescue and reunion with Asha, Tali lives in the Grove, grows, plays, and forms connections across species. Tali embodies the future the restoration effort is meant to protect.

### Ruun — the elder wolf leader

Ruun embodies tradition, discipline, and responsibility, but also the danger of remaining in power too long. His disappearance divides the wolves. Chained beneath a temple, he transmits Black Sap's influence through his howl. Restoring him can preserve an orderly but conservative pack.

### Luma — the younger wolf leader

Luma embodies change, flexibility, and adaptation. Her conflict with Ruun's followers is a disagreement about survival as much as authority. Choosing her leadership produces a more flexible pack with stronger scouting traditions.

### Sable — the wolf companion and envoy

Sable joins Aren after the wolf settlement. Sable is not interchangeable with Ruun or Luma: the latter are political leaders, while Sable accompanies Aren. Tracking, flanking, holding targets, warning of ambushes, and finding secret paths express the pack's strengths.

### Nyra — the misunderstood panther

Nyra is accused of killing animals indiscriminately. In the Bible's account, she has been hunting infected creatures to prevent the corruption from spreading. Aren must examine the evidence rather than accept the accusation at face value. The production clarification about irreversible damage contextualizes these past losses without turning lethal combat into a player option.

Nyra represents instinct and hidden truth. She moves through concealed routes, detects hidden enemies, ambushes from behind, and supports counters after a Perfect Dodge. Ghor, not Nyra, is the boss of Mistveil Basin.

### Solkar — the lion king

Solkar is not simply controlled by Black Sap. He knowingly uses it to preserve his order. His realm has no war, water is managed, territories are assigned, tribute is compulsory, and dissenters are exiled. He is a mirror of what Aren could become: a leader who mistakes obedience for peace.

Solkar can remain as a leader who renounces fear, be exiled in favor of a council, or be subjected to Aren's Dominion. His personal companion abilities include calming wildlife and supporting a group with his roar. Council representation after exile satisfies Crown's political role; it does not create a replacement lion combat companion or secretly summon Solkar.

### Orun — the Hollow King

Once a compassionate Warden, Orun now seeks to remove Erya's capacity to destroy itself. His losses during the Red Summer transformed responsibility into an obsession with control. He does not want to annihilate the forest; he wants to save it by abolishing choice. Aren's gathered fragments and Warden connection give him a path back.

### Marek — the hunter

Marek exploits Black Sap to make traps, control animals, sell energy, and extract resources from a weakened forest. He escapes the bamboo camp with a sample. In Mistveil, Aren may cleanse and save him or leave him behind. If saved, he can reveal information about Heartroot Hollow and later dismantle hunting infrastructure. His survival is not required for the Living Pact; an alternate information route must remain available.

### Ghor — the Mire Maw

Ghor is a giant crocodile transformed by the pollution feeding the Black River. The encounter spans land and water and gives the pollution a visible, dangerous consequence. Ghor is a regional boss, not a recruitable member of the six companion groups. Its resolution follows the same nonlethal cleansing mandate.

## 5. Echo communication

Animals do not converse like humans and do not receive human speech lip-sync. Echo Sense communicates images, scents, sounds, bodily sensations, emotions, and short concepts. At important moments, the game may express that experience as a short translated phrase, not an extended animal monologue.

When Aren touches Asha, the Echo combines an iron cage, the scent of ash, Tali's voice, a trap, and anger. The existing phrase is:

> CHILD — TAKEN — BURNING SCENT

When Aren touches Vara's fragment in the prologue, the vision includes the Root Crown, six pairs of eyes, Orun covered in roots, and a hand reaching from below. The existing phrase is:

> BRING THEM HOME

Solkar's existing philosophical lines are preserved in the dialogue inventory as the meaning conveyed by Echo Sense. Their final presentation must use image, sound, emotion, and brief translated phrasing rather than long human-style animal speech. No additional animal phrases or replacement speeches are authored here.

## 6. Campaign

### Prologue — The Night of a Thousand Roars

**Setting:** Greenwake, at the edge of Erya.

**Opening:** Yara recalls the Wild Pact over images of six leaders gathered around Heartroot, a Warden setting down a staff, Orun wearing the Root Crown, Heartroot cracking, and Black Sap erupting.

**Story beats and objectives:**

1. Experience the night when every creature roars at once, birds flee their nests, wolves leave the mountains, and frightened animals surge through the settlement.
2. Reach Vara after she falls before Aren with the Sky fragment.
3. Touch the fragment and witness the Crown, the six pairs of eyes, Orun within the roots, and the plea to bring them home.
4. Receive the Warden Staff from Yara.
5. Move through Greenwake, evade the animals, learn the introductory movement and staff techniques, and cleanse a corrupted boar without killing it.
6. Discover that the animals are fleeing something beneath the forest, not waging war on the settlement.

**Fragment ledger:** Sky; one fragment in total.

The Bible does not supply individual quest titles for this prologue. These objectives adapt its existing sequence rather than inventing additional named quests.

### Act I — The Forest Without a Voice

### Chapter 1 — Whispering Canopy

**Setting:** Layered tropical forest, rope bridges, waterfalls, a small temple, bird nests, and intersecting ground and canopy routes.

**Conflict:** Monkeys steal food from birds. The birds hold the last seeds. The orchard is dying because Black Sap has poisoned its water, and hunters have trapped the source. Neither community is entirely wrong.

#### Main quest: The Fallen Scout

**Objectives:** Tend to Vara. Learn Bird Mark. Learn to recognize her warning of attacks outside Aren's view.

**Story beat:** Care for the creature that brought Sky before asking her to guide the journey. Her scouting becomes the first practical expression of cooperation.

#### Main quest: Thief in the Branches

**Objectives:** Pursue Koro and recover the stolen Heartroot fragment. Vault obstacles, evade branches, slide beneath roots, move between foreground and background paths, and follow the vine route.

**Story beat:** Koro is testing Aren, not merely stealing for its own sake. In the curated First Pact slice, any assistance agreed during the chase is explicitly provisional; it does not imply that Koro has already been cleansed or fully bonded.

#### Main quest: The Empty Orchard

**Objectives:** Restore the water flow. Rescue trapped monkeys. Bring seeds from the birds. Discover the Black Sap thorn in Koro's shoulder.

**Story beat:** Repairing the orchard addresses the reason both communities are desperate. The poisoned thorn explains Koro's intensified aggression without erasing his community's legitimate needs.

**Boss resolution — Koro, Branchbreaker:** Read rock throws, falling branches, platform charges, leaps between depth bands, and horizontal log sweeps. Use movement, Vara's information, and the staff to create an opening. Pull out the Black Sap thorn with Root Tether; do not kill Koro.

**Outcome:** Koro shares seeds with the birds, Canopy joins Sky, and Koro becomes a utility companion. Birds return, monkeys build bridges, the orchard regrows, and a route to the Grove opens. The production plan treats the water-and-seed accord as a lasting obligation, not a reward granted solely for winning the fight.

**Fragment ledger:** Sky + Canopy; two fragments.

### Chapter 2 — Blood Bamboo Valley

**Setting:** A red-lit bamboo valley with an ancient shrine, a hunting camp, dense leaves, and deliberately obscured sightlines.

**Conflict:** Hunters have taken Tali. Black Sap traps amplify Asha's anger until she attacks without distinguishing friend from foe.

#### Main quest: Tracks Without Blood

**Objectives:** Follow broken bamboo, crushed traps, Tali's fur, footprints, and the hunters' scent.

**Story beat:** Read evidence of a mother's search rather than treating the damage as proof of a mindless monster.

#### Main quest: The Ash Hunters

**Objectives:** Reach Marek's camp. Subdue its hunters nonlethally, disable the camp, and rescue Tali.

**Story beat:** Marek escapes with a Black Sap sample. Tali's main rescue happens here; the later side quest does not repeat this rescue.

#### Main quest: A Mother's Roar

**Objectives:** Follow Tali into the bamboo. Survive Asha's attacks, protect the cub, restrain Asha long enough to approach, and cleanse her.

**Story beat:** Asha appears but cannot recognize Tali. Aren must restore her ability to protect, not defeat her right to resist.

**Boss — Asha, the Broken Fang:**

1. **Stalking:** Shaking bamboo, startled birds, flattened grass, a tiger's silhouette, and positional sound reveal her movement. Read Tiger Pounce, Claw Combo, and Side Charge; evade their direction and timing.
2. **Corruption Rage:** Roar Cone, Black Sap Trail, Corrupted Pounce, and Ground Corruption Wave make the corruption visible. Vara can mark the landing. Movement and vaulting create a path through the attacks.
3. **The Cub:** Asha prepares to charge Tali. Evade Bamboo Charge, ask Koro to drop the bamboo bundle, use Root Tether, approach, and cleanse. Tali reaches Asha, who recognizes the cub again. The production plan guarantees provisional Koro cooperation for this authored interaction in the slice; interaction failure resets or retries rather than permanently removing the required help.

**Outcome:** Receive Fang. Asha becomes a combat companion, and Tali moves to the Grove. Failure at a mandatory Tali-protection beat means a checkpoint retry, not an unannounced permanent cub death.

**Fragment ledger:** Sky + Canopy + Fang; **three fragments**. Orun's corrected existing line is: “Three have returned. Three remain.”

### The Grove — a sanctuary that records the journey

The Grove begins with a small tree, a pool, and Yara's tent. It supports companion selection, upgrades, evasion practice, memory viewing, side quests, and observation of relationships between species.

Its progression makes restoration visible:

- **Early sanctuary:** Vara and Koro arrive; birds nest and monkeys build platforms.
- **After Asha:** Asha has a den; Tali lives and plays nearby.
- **After the wolves:** A wolf den, training area, and trail appear.
- **After Mistveil:** Nyra gains a shaded area; the water system and small wildlife recover.
- **After Sunmane:** A lion terrace and representatives of the regions give the hub a wider political life. An exile branch does not imply Solkar's presence.
- **Chapter 6:** Orun destroys the sanctuary. The production plan moves essential services to a refuge rather than stranding the campaign.
- **Epilogue:** The Grove becomes a Wild King throne, a free sanctuary, or the Circle of the Wild, according to the chosen ending.

### Act II — War of Teeth and Claws

### Chapter 3 — Moonpine Ridge

**Setting:** Pine forest, cold mountains, fog, a frozen lake, wolf caves, and stone ruins.

**Conflict:** Ruun has disappeared. His traditionalist followers and Luma's reformers are splitting the pack.

#### Main quest: The Third Howl

**Objective:** Follow a three-beat howl rising from beneath the ground.

**Story beat:** The missing leader's voice remains connected to the pack even while his location is hidden.

#### Main quest: Two Packs

**Objectives:** Help one or both factions protect their pups, find food, rescue scouts, and prevent civil war.

**Story beat:** Learn what each generation fears and needs before deciding how the pack should be led.

#### Main quest: The Alpha Below

**Objectives:** Reach Ruun beneath the temple. Break the restraints and confront the corruption spreading through his howl. Cleanse him and resolve the leadership conflict.

**Story beat:** Ruun is chained below the community he once led, and his connection to the pack has become a conduit for Black Sap.

**Boss resolution — Ruun, the Chained Howl:** Navigate multi-lane Pack Charges, Howl Shockwaves, flanks, breaking ice, and Chain Pounces. Asha can disrupt formations, Koro can handle chains, and Vara can identify the real Ruun. Aren can break anchors and evade without requiring a particular optional companion loadout.

**Settlement choices:**

- **Restore Ruun:** Preserve disciplined but conservative leadership.
- **Choose Luma:** Establish more flexible leadership with stronger scouting.
- **Dominion:** Force both factions to submit, gaining immediate power while increasing vulnerability to Orun and permanently disqualifying Living Pact in this run.

**Outcome:** Sable joins as the pack's companion envoy. Receive Pack. A vision reveals that Yara helped Orun create the Root Crown, and Aren confronts her.

**Fragment ledger:** Sky + Canopy + Fang + Pack; four fragments.

### Chapter 4 — Mistveil Basin

**Setting:** Wetlands, dense fog, submerged ruins, great root trees, luminous animals, and a river carrying Black Sap.

**Conflict:** Nyra is blamed for animal deaths. The evidence points to her attempt to contain infection and to the corruption's actual source.

#### Main quest: Eyes in the Mist

**Objectives:** Investigate the watcher in the mist. Follow eyes in the fog, silhouettes in the trees, moving leaves, and tracks that vanish. Examine the truth behind the accusations against Nyra.

**Story beat:** Suspicion must give way to observation. Nyra is not an additional boss to defeat.

#### Main quest: The Black River

**Objectives:** Trace the pollution to Marek's Black Sap operation. Confront him and choose whether to cleanse and save him or leave him behind.

**Story beat:** Marek contrasts Aren's requests for help with his own ability to force obedience. Saving him can create a later source of information and practical assistance; refusing does not block the Hollow route or automatically disqualify Living Pact.

#### Main quest: The Mire Maw

**Objectives:** Reach the polluted source, confront Ghor, cleanse the corruption, and restore the river.

**Story beat:** The giant crocodile's transformation is a consequence of exploitation upstream, not proof that the swamp itself is evil.

**Boss resolution — Ghor, the Mire Maw:** Fight across land and water. Read Tail Sweep, Water Charge, Mud Eruption, Bite Lunge, and Black River Wave. Vara can reveal Ghor underwater and Nyra can create an opening, but ground signs and sound must remain sufficient without optional assistance.

**Outcome:** The river is cleansed, Shadow is recovered, and Nyra joins. Yara admits that she believed in Orun's plan and understood its cost too late. Restoring the river and acknowledging Nyra's truth are distinct from merely ending the fight.

**Fragment ledger:** Sky + Canopy + Fang + Pack + Shadow; five fragments.

### Act III — The False King

### Chapter 5 — Sunmane Expanse

**Setting:** Grassland with scattered trees, a great river, cliffs, stone temples, and tightly regulated herds.

**Conflict:** Solkar has made a peaceful realm through fear. Resources and territory are assigned, herbivores pay tribute, and dissenters are expelled. Peace has become immobility.

#### Main quest: The Silent Plains

**Objectives:** Investigate the unnatural calm. Observe the restrictions preventing animals from moving freely.

**Story beat:** An absence of visible conflict does not prove a just settlement.

#### Main quest: Tribute of Teeth

**Objective:** Confront the tribute imposed on a herd of antelope, whose young are demanded by the lions.

**Story beat:** Reveal the price of Solkar's order. The threat belongs to the story; it does not authorize graphic harm or lethal player action.

#### Main quest: The Lion's Offer

**Objectives:** Hear the meaning of Solkar's offer through Echo Sense. Confront his claim that Aren also gathers power under a gentler name. Face him and decide how Sunmane will be governed.

**Story beat:** Solkar is an ideological challenge, not merely another corrupted beast.

**Boss — Solkar, the Sunmane King:**

1. **Trial of Strength:** Read Claw Sweep, Shoulder Charge, Leap Slam, and Roar Cone.
2. **The King's Army:** Disrupt coordinated defenders. Available allies can reveal commanders, break signals, hold guards, attack from behind, and face Solkar directly. Aren retains cover and signal-breaking routes without a full companion roster.
3. **Dominion Roar:** Fear and coercion test relationships. Vulnerable companions may falter or turn against Aren during signaled possession windows. Protection and cleansing remain nonlethal.

**Settlement choices:**

- **Bond:** Solkar continues to lead but renounces control through fear.
- **Exile:** Solkar leaves; the herds form a council. A willing council representative can represent Crown thereafter.
- **Dominion:** Aren compels Solkar's obedience, with the permanent historical consequence for Living Pact.

**Outcome:** Receive Crown. The six fragments gather around Aren. Orun thanks Aren for returning his kingdom to one hand.

**Fragment ledger:** All six unique fragments; no additional fragment is awarded for a council representative.

### Chapter 6 — The Fall of the Grove

**Opening:** Aren returns to the Grove, where Yara prepares a sealing ritual. Instead of entering Heartroot, the fragments enter the mark on Aren's hand. Aren is the key the Root Crown requires.

**Story beats and objectives:**

1. Witness roots break through the ground as Orun returns.
2. Protect rather than kill companions whose connections Orun has seized. Evade attacks from the allies actually present; do not insert an exiled Solkar into the scene.
3. Use temporary Cleanse Pulses, rescue Tali, and destroy the Black Sap pillar. Stronger Bonds let companions resist sooner.
4. Witness Yara sever the connection between Orun and Aren. Roots encase her as a living wooden statue.
5. Survive the Grove's collapse as Orun takes the Heartroot Core and disappears.
6. Lose coercive Warden's Call, but retain the ability to Ask and Signal willing allies.

**Emotional turn:** Aren admits they can no longer command. Vara remains. Other companions' return or conditions depend on relationship history; they do not all automatically forget coercion. Yara's answer reframes the journey: ask.

**Continuity:** Essential services move to the refuge. Mandatory protection failures retry; missing optional support changes the route, not the ability to finish the campaign.

The Bible gives this chapter a sequence, not additional named main quests.

### Act IV — The March of the Wild

### Chapter 7 — The Living Pact

The chapter retains its original title, but its events form the **Wild March coalition**, not an automatic Living Pact ending. Aren revisits all five regions without the power to force cooperation. Each community must decide whether to join. The coalition can be incomplete; forming it neither awards the ending nor erases Dominion history.

The following **five named return beats come from the production plan**, expanding the Bible's regional recommitment outline. They are new objectives, not repeat boss fights.

#### Return beat 1: Seeds on the Wind — Whispering Canopy

**Objectives:** Coordinate birds and monkeys carrying seeds past root-blocked routes. Use a living bridge where the orchard has been restored; otherwise repair the water source before expecting its restoration benefit. Seek their voluntary recommitment.

**Payoff:** Koro's community responds to actual recovery. Birds and monkeys demonstrate cooperation instead of competing over the last seeds. Sky and Canopy remain distinct factions even though they share a region.

#### Return beat 2: A Path for the Young — Blood Bamboo Valley

**Objectives:** Escort Tali and the nursery along an evacuation route with remaining traps. Fulfill the safety obligations that allow Asha to choose her role.

**Payoff:** Asha chooses whether to protect the evacuation or lead the advance according to completed obligations. Her willingness is rooted in the protection of the young, not a renewed command from Aren.

#### Return beat 3: One Howl, Two Generations — Moonpine Ridge

**Objectives:** Establish communication signals and assign roles between the two generations of wolves. Request renewed cooperation under the settlement Aren helped create.

**Payoff:** Ruun, Luma, or a history of Dominion changes the formation and available cooperation, not only a line of dialogue. Leadership and Sable's companion role remain separate.

#### Return beat 4: Witness in the Reeds — Mistveil Basin

**Objectives:** Open a less-corrupted corridor. Follow Nyra's route markings. If Marek survived and is available to help, use his knowledge to dismantle the camp mechanism; otherwise take the existing alternate route.

**Payoff:** The truth about Nyra and the treatment of Marek have visible consequences. Neither Marek nor his optional quest becomes a hidden requirement for finishing the story.

#### Return beat 5: The Open Crossing — Sunmane Expanse

**Objectives:** Free a migration passage. Obtain voluntary support through Solkar's acceptance of limits or the council's shared authority.

**Payoff:** The plains demonstrate a form of leadership that permits movement and refusal. Council representation supplies Crown's political commitment without inventing a replacement combat companion.

**The march:** At Heartroot Hollow, willing leaders step forward without Aren wearing a crown or issuing orders. According to the coalition actually assembled, birds gather overhead, monkeys travel the roots, tigers advance, wolves take the flanks, panthers move through shadow, and lions and plains animals approach. Absent support must remain absent from the scene and from later assists.

**Outcome:** The **Wild March coalition** enters the final campaign. The permanent **Circle of the Wild** is reserved for an eligible player who chooses the Living Pact ending.

### Chapter 8 — Heartroot Hollow

**Setting:** Giant roots, streams of Black Sap, memories across the walls, Warden ruins, and routes shaped by the world state.

**Objectives and story beats:**

1. Enter through the routes the coalition has made available.
2. Where those allies are present, birds attack Black Sap Spires, Koro opens a root gate, Asha and the wolves hold the main route, and Nyra guides Aren through darkness. Solkar's assistance is possible only if his branch and relationship permit it; council representation does not imply his presence.
3. Follow alternate player routes when optional support is absent.
4. Experience Orun's memories: a good Warden who lost his family and the creatures he protected during the Red Summer.
5. Confront his conclusion that sharing failed because choice itself is the wound.
6. Reach the final confrontation and determine the fate of the Root Crown.

The Bible does not provide additional named main quests for this chapter. Its existing sequence and the three named final-boss phases are retained rather than expanded into invented quest titles.

## 7. Final boss — three phases, one confrontation

### Phase 1 — Orun, the Last Warden

Orun uses a distorted reflection of Aren's own skills: Root Tether, staff combinations, Cartwheel Counter, Back Handspring, Dive Roll, Ground Root Wave, Perfect Dodge, and companion suppression.

**Objective:** Read Orun's movement and counter him rather than relying on allies to fight in Aren's place. The production plan treats this as an authored mirror encounter, not limitless input-reading. His accusation contrasts Aren's protection by others with his own isolation.

### Phase 2 — The Crowned Beast

Orun fuses with Heartroot into a being carrying the six species' powers.

## 8. Endings and epilogues

### Shared ending rules

The Bible defines three endings. The approved historical Dominion rule is absolute for the current run. The master plan supplies the following **detailed production predicate**, including proposed tier and obligation details, to make the Bible's broad requirements explicit:

- No leader has ever been subjected to Dominion in this run.
- All six factions have reached covenant tier 3 and have a voluntary representative. A Crown council representative is valid after Solkar's exile.
- All five major regions are restored and the Hollow resolution is complete.
- The five regional obligations are fulfilled:
  - **Canopy:** the water-and-seed accord.
  - **Bamboo:** Tali is safe and the snare source is neutralized.
  - **Moonpine:** the pack's civil-war conflict is settled.
  - **Mistveil:** the river's pollution source is neutralized and Nyra's truth is acknowledged.
  - **Sunmane:** coercive tribute has ended and governance is voluntary.
- All six groups have recommitted during Chapter 7.

Marek's survival and completion of The Hunter's Remorse are **not** requirements. Political settlement and ecological restoration are separate matters; winning an election or cleansing a boss does not alone establish a restored region.

These conditions make Living Pact available as a final option; they do not select it for the player. Improving Bond after Dominion does not restore eligibility. Dominion and Severance are still available whether Living Pact is eligible or not.

### Ending 1 — Dominion: The Wild King

**Final choice:** Aren accepts the Root Crown.

**Epilogue:** Conflict ends. Every animal bows, and the forest falls silent. Birds wait for a command before they fly. Aren's shadow resembles Orun's. The Grove becomes the Wild King's throne.

The ending preserves Orun's promise of order while exposing its cost: the forest lives, but its creatures no longer decide how to live together.

### Ending 2 — Severance: The Free Wild

**Final choice:** Aren destroys the Root Crown.

**Epilogue:** The six fragments return to their regions. Erya remains imperfect: animals hunt, disputes continue, and no one can command the whole forest. Companionship continues by choice rather than compulsion. The Grove becomes a free sanctuary.

The account of voluntary followers must reflect who is actually present and willing, not forcibly restore every possible companion. The sources do not specify Yara's or Orun's individual fate in this ending; this adaptation does not invent one.

### Ending 3 — The Living Pact

**Final choice:** With the full predicate satisfied, Aren divides the Root Crown into six parts and establishes the Circle of the Wild.

**Epilogue:** Orun relinquishes power. Yara is restored to life. There is no throne. Six paths lead to six territories, and each species retains the right to join or leave. The Grove becomes the Circle of the Wild, a place of representation rather than ownership.

Yara asks what they will call Aren now. Aren answers that they do not need to call them anything. The ending completes the journey from gathering power to making room for others' choices.

Regional epilogues should express the settlements and restoration outcomes already earned. The sources provide these ending beats, not complete branching vignette scripts or a separate fate for every named character in every ending.

1. **Not a full voiceover script:** The sources contain selected lines and story outlines. Those are future English dialogue-authoring work, not silently completed by this adaptation.
2. **Solkar's Echo staging:** His existing sentences carry the intended meaning. The final segmentation into short Echo subtitles, images, sound, and emotion remains unscripted; human-style animal monologues are not approved by preserving the source lines.
3. **Detailed ending implementation:** The strict no-historical-Dominion rule is approved. Covenant tier 3, the exact obligation ledger, and its final scene timing are the master plan's detailed predicate baseline, originally marked for narrative sign-off. “Hollow resolution complete” must be defined consistently with the final-choice point; this document does not invent its event trigger.
4. **Optional quest granularity:** The Bible supplies brief summaries, and the five production proposals are even less detailed. This document translates all supplied objectives and consequences without inventing recipients, numeric allocations, dialogue, or encounter scripts that do not exist in the sources.
5. **Nyra's history:** The plan proposes the recoverable-versus-irreversible distinction to reconcile her past actions with cleansing. The exact evidence and memories establishing that distinction remain unscripted. No arbitrary lethal exception for Aren is introduced.
6. **Ending-specific fates:** Yara's restoration and Orun's relinquishment of power are specified for Living Pact. Their individual outcomes in the other endings are not supplied and are not invented here.
7. **Trailer chronology:** The retained trailer opening associates the mass cry with Heartroot's shattering, while the campaign distinguishes the earlier failed ritual from the present Greenwake disturbance. This is existing promotional language, not a new timeline decision; its chronology needs editorial resolution before use as literal campaign narration.
8. **Plan clarifications retain provenance:** The shared Sky/Canopy region, separation of companion and political representatives, Last Cub reconciliation, refuge continuity, absence-safe routes, and detailed side-quest consequences are identified as production-plan clarifications rather than falsely attributed to original Bible prose.
